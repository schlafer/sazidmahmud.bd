Every engineer I had talked to, have one thing common in them; all of them want to take up farming as a hobby turned into profession at some point in their life. Be it software, semiconductor or electrical, no matter what their field of engineering is, all of them have this latent desire to take up farming as a job and leave their field for good. 

Now is this a delusion? In short Yes. Most engineers are not rightly fitted for the physical workload of farming. They are daydreaming about the outcome not the tasks you need to do to get to that point. Farming takes tremendous toll on the physique. The risks involved are real. Especially dealing with livestock (no rollback!)

So from where this universal delusion emerge? Isolation. Most engineering jobs are memory intensive as well as hard on the human psyche. Engineers mostly work in workstations in a cubicle. Most of the socializing is about meetings, deadlines and layoffs. Engineers have become modern day equivalent of slaves, where their whole existence is measured through their productivity. This sense of alienation results in yearning for something more fulfilling as humans.

But is this delusion bad? Not at all. In my opinion, ruminating about doing physical labor instead of crunching numbers in spreadsheets has positive effect. It gives you hope. You think, "Well this sucks, I have to do a lot of work which I'm not enjoying at the moment. But if I were to become a farmer, I could do this and that and so on". Hope is all we have in this life. Without hope everything is meaningless. Very nihilistic.

If they were to test that delusion and find out it didn't entertain their hope, it would be even more miserable.
